USB tether control: limitations
===============================

How it works: a USB cable from the PC plugs straight into the drone's flight
controller, and the PC sends stick commands down the cable.

1. The cable fights the drone (the main problem)
   - The cable plugs into the back of the drone, so its weight and pull tip
     the drone backwards. One motor has to work noticeably harder just to stay
     level.
   - When the cable goes tight, the flight controller keeps pushing harder
     against it. When the pull suddenly lets go, that built-up push makes the
     drone flip.
   - A setting called airmode helps in one way and hurts in another: with it
     off, the drone loses control when the throttle drops in the air; with it
     on, it surges against the cable.
   - Even a light USB-C cable is heavy for a 20 g drone. Three thin wires
     soldered to the board's spare serial pads would be much lighter.
   - The USB connection dropped mid-flight several times, which cuts control.

2. The drone cannot tell where it is
   - It only has a gyro and an accelerometer. It knows which way is level,
     but not its position or height, so it always drifts and needs constant
     correction. Drones that hover in place on their own use a downward camera
     and a height sensor, which this one does not have.
   - In flight, motor vibration swamps the accelerometer, so the software
     cannot work out how fast the drone is rising or falling.
   - The flight-assist mode can only guess speed from how far the drone is
     tilted. It cannot feel the cable or air pushing it.

3. Small rooms are rough air
   - The drone's own downdraft bounces off the floor and walls and shoves it
     around, causing sudden darts.

4. Setup problems found along the way (now fixed)
   - The flight controller was smoothing the controls so heavily that throttle
     changes took seconds to arrive; the drone seemed too weak to lift.
   - Its sense of "level" was about 6 degrees off, so it tipped over instead of
     climbing until it was recalibrated.

5. Hard to control height
   - The drone hovers at a fairly low throttle, inside a narrow band on a short
     gamepad trigger, so tiny finger movements make big height changes.

6. Limited flight records
   - The drone's onboard flight log memory is full, so the only records are
     the lower-detail logs the PC saves.
